SmolVLA — a visual walkthrough

Why SmolVLA is the cheapest fix for the 50% target-color ceiling we hit with ACT on Eval 2.

1. The problem we're solving

We trained 4 ACT models on the same 101-episode dataset, varying only image augmentation and training steps. All four plateaued at the same ~50% target-color obedience on the real SO-101. That's not a training-quality issue — it's random with respect to color choice, which is exactly what an architectural gap looks like.

ACT in lerobot is vision-only. It receives image + proprioception, nothing else. The task-string field single_task is stored as dataset metadata and never reaches the model. So even though we labeled every episode with "Pick yellow block and place in bowl at (-15.5,29.5) cm", the model could not condition on it.

Key takeaway: the 50% ceiling is the architecture, not the data. Same data, different architecture (SmolVLA) is the cheapest test.

2. How SmolVLA actually works

SmolVLA (Shukor et al. 2026, arXiv:2506.01844) is a 450M-parameter vision-language-action model built around three encoders that converge into a cross-attention transformer:

  • Vision encoder — SigLIP-style image embeddings. Trained on a huge multi-task robot-learning corpus.
  • Proprio MLP — small projection that turns joint angles into a token sequence the transformer can attend to.
  • Text encoder — SmolLM-base. Tokenizes natural language task strings and turns them into embeddings.

The three streams meet in a cross-attention transformer that fuses them into a unified hidden representation, then a small action head emits chunks of joint targets (similar to ACT, but conditioned on all three modalities).

Crucially, SmolVLA is pretrained. The 450M params already encode generic robot-learning priors from hundreds of thousands of demonstration trajectories. We fine-tune those weights on our 101 episodes — we are not training from scratch. This is why the recipe in the playbook is so cheap: 20k steps, batch 64, ~4 hours on an A100.

3. What this means for the 2-cube task

In Eval 1 there was one cube. "Pick a cube" was unambiguous. ACT, despite having no instruction input, still hit 5/5 because every demonstration in the dataset pointed at the same kind of action.

In Eval 2 there are two cubes. "Pick a cube" is now ambiguous — which one? The instruction is the disambiguating signal. Without it, the policy averages, which on a balanced 2-color dataset means ~50% by construction.

4. What we should expect — published numbers

The SmolVLA paper (Table 3 in arXiv:2506.01844) benchmarks both architectures on SO-100/SO-101 pick-place tasks. Community reproductions exist with substantial variance (Habuda, Saroha, Sawane, Kamath all report different numbers — data hygiene dominates).

Reading the right chart: our 4 ACT runs are tied at 50% because the task is genuinely not learnable for them in clutter — no matter how much augmentation we add. SmolVLA's expected range is 70–90% in-distribution, but the community variance (visible as the wide error bar) is the risk we accept.

5. How we'd actually do it

The training script we'd send to the 5090 box looks like the standard lerobot finetune recipe with a different policy type. We don't change the dataset, the camera setup, the bowl position, or the deploy script. We change one thing: the model.

6. Risks worth naming

Risk What goes wrong Mitigation
Camera-name mismatch SmolVLA pretraining expects specific camera keys (top + wrist). Our dataset uses wrist only. If the key naming differs, pretrained visual weights don't load and we're effectively training from scratch. Check lerobot/smolvla_base model card before training. Rename camera in features mapping if needed (no dataset mutation).
Wrist-only vs top+wrist SmolVLA pretraining is dominated by top + wrist views. Wrist-only is documented to underperform by 10–20 percentage points. Accept this; no top camera is available on our SO-101 mid-project. Compare result against the lower end of the published range, not the upper.
Community reproduction variance LeRobot issue #2915: 0% with 120 SO-101 episodes. Same recipe, different result — usually a data-hygiene issue. Validate single-batch inference output before committing to full training.
Blackwell sm_120 on the 5090 PyTorch wheel mismatch can cause CUDA kernel errors on training. Pin torch==2.9.1+cu128 if needed (CLAUDE.md flags this).

7. The decision this visualization is meant to inform

The playbook recommends two ways to add color conditioning to ACT-class policies: SmolVLA finetune (cheap, no surgery) or FiLM-conditioned ACT (more invasive). SmolVLA is week-1 priority because the marginal cost is one Brev H100 finetune (~$8) and the upside is a usable primitive for HIL-SERL refinement in weeks 2–4.

If SmolVLA still misses target color (down at the wrist-only-failed end of the range), the next escalation is FiLM-ACT. That's a separate experiment with separate risk profile — but it would happen only after this one definitively rules out language conditioning.
